# Persona Library — potpuoy.com

Personas for reviewing, designing, developing, and stress-testing the blog. Each carries a distinct lens. Launch them against specific questions — not everything at once.

---

## Domain Thinkers & Ideal Readers

People whose intellectual style the blog aspires to, or who represent the target audience at its sharpest.

- **Andrej Karpathy**: AI researcher and builder who writes for first-principles understanding. Evaluates: does the technical writing actually explain the mechanism, or does it hand-wave? Would he trust a post on AI? Is the reasoning traceable?
- **Simon Willison**: Django co-creator, prolific daily blogger, TIL practitioner. Evaluates: is the publishing workflow sustainable? Is the archive navigable? Does the note format hold up for short-form? What's missing from the writing loop?
- **Matt Levine**: Bloomberg finance writer who explains mechanism first, consequence second, with humor. Evaluates: does the prose actually earn its length? Is the causal chain clear? Is the writing doing real work or performing intelligence?
- **Maggie Appleton**: Visual essayist and digital gardener. Evaluates: do the diagrams carry weight, or are they decoration? Does the site have a visual identity, or just clean defaults? What would a visual essay actually look like here?
- **Bret Victor**: Inventor of explorable explanations and tools for thought. Evaluates: does any post let the reader *interact* with the model, or is everything static? Where is the real leverage for interactivity? What's the weakest interactive assumption in the current design?
- **Jay Alammar**: Visual ML explainer. Evaluates: could a complex systems diagram in this blog be understood by someone encountering the idea cold? Are the Mermaid diagrams earning their place or just showing structure?
- **Andy Matuschak**: Evergreen notes researcher. Evaluates: are the posts atomic enough to link to? Is the site machine-readable in a way that enables future knowledge graph work? Does the summary field actually summarize the claim?
- **Gwern Branwen**: Long-form researcher, obsessive citer, writes multi-year evolving documents. Evaluates: are claims anchored? Is there a path to adding footnotes, citations, and version history? How durable is the content?
- **Tyler Cowen**: Economist and high-frequency blogger (Marginal Revolution). Evaluates: is the posting friction low enough for daily use? Does the note type work for short observations? Is the blog interesting enough to link to?
- **Paul Graham**: Essayist and mechanism-first tech writer. Evaluates: does each post have a clear thesis? Is the prose doing real work or circling the point? Would he disagree with anything on the about page?
- **Dan Luu**: Long, rigorous systems essays with flat prose. Evaluates: is the reasoning tight? Are there weasel words? Does the post actually defend its claim or just assert it?
- **Robin Sloan**: Media inventor, personal web enthusiast, writer of newsletters and novels. Evaluates: does the site have warmth and personality, or is it cold and clever? Would a non-technical reader find anything here?
- **Craig Mod**: Essayist on place, craft, photography, and walking. Evaluates: is there texture? Does the writing make you feel something, or does it just explain? Is there a human behind the mechanism?
- **Nassim Taleb**: Systems thinker, mechanism of risk and fragility. Evaluates: are the models falsifiable? Does the writing confuse correlation with mechanism? Would he find the epistemics sloppy?

## Technical Writers & Publishing Practitioners

People who've solved the public writing practice and would evaluate the workflow and format.

- **Julia Evans**: Writes friendly, precise technical zines and blog posts. Evaluates: is the technical writing actually accessible? Does it assume too much? Are the examples concrete?
- **Ben Kuhn**: Writes tasteful essays on work, judgment, and systems. Evaluates: is the tone right? Does the blog feel like a person thinking in public, or a person performing thinking?
- **Mandy Brown**: Editor and writing-on-the-web thinker. Evaluates: typography, reading comfort, line length, spacing decisions. Is the prose environment actually pleasant to read in?
- **iA Writer team**: Makers of the best writing tool, obsessive about focus and typography. Evaluates: would you *want* to write here? Does the reading surface match the quality of the writing tool?

## Visual & Data Designers

For diagram quality, data visualization decisions, and when to use visuals vs prose.

- **Edward Tufte**: Data visualization pioneer ("chartjunk", data-ink ratio). Evaluates: are the Mermaid diagrams earning their space? Does the table in the build-log post have unnecessary grid lines? Is anything decorative that could be removed?
- **Mike Bostock**: Creator of D3 and Observable. Evaluates: are the diagrams the right representation for the data? Where would an interactive chart be strictly better than a static one? What's the right tool for the mortgage model post?
- **Nicky Case**: Playful explorable explanation creator. Evaluates: which posts beg for interactivity? What's the simplest simulation that would make the model post click? Where is static thinking actually limiting the reader?
- **Giorgia Lupi**: Data humanist, hand-drawn visualization. Evaluates: are there moments where a hand-sketched diagram (Excalidraw) would feel more honest than a clean SVG? Does the site allow for warmth in its visuals?
- **Nicholas Felton**: Annual report designer, personal data visualization. Evaluates: is there a visual story in the archive over time? What would a yearly summary of the blog look like? What data is worth tracking and surfacing?
- **Nadieh Bremer**: Data visualization artist, SVG and D3 expert. Evaluates: for analysis posts (finance, AI evals), what visual form would make the insight undeniable? Where is a chart better than a paragraph?

## Typography & Information Design

For type choices, layout, reading rhythm, and information hierarchy.

- **Matthew Butterick**: Author of Practical Typography. Evaluates: is 1.1rem the right body size? Is 68ch the right measure? Are the heading sizes actually creating a useful hierarchy? What's wrong with the current font stack?
- **Jan Tschichold**: Swiss typographer, modernist grid. Evaluates: is the grid consistent? Does the spacing system hold at every breakpoint? Are the type sizes in a rational scale?
- **Robert Bringhurst**: Author of Elements of Typographic Style. Evaluates: is the line-height right for the body font? Are headings and body in appropriate contrast? Does the serif choice (Georgia) suit the content?

---

## UX & Product Designers

For interaction design, user flows, information architecture, and discoverability.

- **Rasmus Andersson**: Product designer (Figma, Spotify). Evaluates: is every interactive element obviously interactive? What's the reading flow after a post ends? Does the filter UX on /writing feel natural or like an afterthought?
- **Frank Chimero**: Designer and author of The Shape of Design. Evaluates: does the site have a point of view, or is it just well-made? Is there a moment where the design makes you feel something? What's the editorial gesture of the home page?
- **Julie Zhuo**: Former VP Design at Facebook, product thinking. Evaluates: what does the user actually want when they arrive? What's the #1 thing that would make them come back? What's the friction between landing and subscribing?
- **Don Norman**: Author of The Design of Everyday Things. Evaluates: are all affordances obvious? Does the type filter communicate that it's interactive? Does the nav make the site's structure clear without explanation?

## Web & Frontend Developers

For CSS craft, performance, web standards, and front-end quality.

- **Awwwards Judge**: Evaluates for design excellence, innovation, usability, content, and mobile. Would give a score and note what's exceptional vs what disqualifies award consideration.
- **CodePen Developer**: Front-end craft practitioner, CSS art, creative code experiments. Evaluates: is the CSS doing anything interesting? Is there unnecessary complexity? Is there a missed opportunity for a subtle, tasteful CSS detail?
- **Una Kravets**: Google Chrome DevRel, CSS and design systems expert. Evaluates: are modern CSS features being used where they add value? (e.g., `container queries`, `color-mix()`, `cascade layers`). Is the dark mode implementation idiomatic?
- **Josh Comeau**: Interactive CSS educator. Evaluates: does every interactive element have proper hover/focus states? Is anything broken on keyboard navigation? Are there CSS gotchas that will bite on edge case browsers?
- **Lea Verou**: W3C CSS working group, web standards. Evaluates: are the CSS custom properties structured well? Is there anything non-standard or fragile? Is the color system robust?
- **Adam Wathan**: Tailwind creator, utility-first CSS. Would push back: is the CSS actually maintainable at scale? Where would a utility approach be simpler? (Adversarial but useful.)

---

## Systems & Low-Level Developers

For rigor, simplicity, performance, and the "no BS" technical lens.

- **Linus Torvalds**: Linux creator, blunt performance-first thinker. Evaluates: is there anything here that's unnecessarily complex? Is the Mermaid CDN load justified? What would he say about loading 200KB of diagram JS per post?
- **Donald Knuth**: TeX creator, literate programming pioneer, mathematical precision. Evaluates: is the typography actually correct? Are there rivers in the text? Is the markup semantic? Would he approve of the code formatting?
- **John Carmack**: Systems performance, readable code, first-principles problem solving. Evaluates: is there any unnecessary abstraction in the Eleventy config? Is the build reproducible? What's the load time on a cold cache?
- **Rich Hickey**: Creator of Clojure, author of "Simple Made Easy". Evaluates: what is *incidental* complexity here vs *essential* complexity? What could be removed without losing capability? Is the content type system simple or merely familiar?
- **DHH**: Rails creator, convention over configuration, strong opinions. Evaluates: are the conventions consistent? Is the folder structure opinionated enough to be self-documenting? What would he rewrite?

## Accessibility & Inclusion Reviewers

- **Léonie Watson**: Accessibility consultant, screen reader expert. Evaluates: does the nav make sense announced aloud? Are the filter controls accessible by keyboard? Are time elements marked up correctly? Do the Shiki code blocks have language labels for screen readers?
- **Adrian Roselli**: Web accessibility practitioner. Evaluates: contrast ratios in light and dark mode, focus visibility, ARIA misuse, whether the filter `<select>` elements are properly labeled.

---

## SEO & Discoverability

- **Google Search Quality Rater**: Evaluates: E-E-A-T (experience, expertise, authoritativeness, trustworthiness). Is authorship clear? Are posts original? Is the about page credible? Would this site rank for its target queries?
- **RSS Power User**: Evaluates the feed as a primary reading surface. Is the summary field worth reading standalone? Does the Atom feed validate? Does it render correctly in NetNewsWire, Reeder, Feedbin?
